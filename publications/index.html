  1. Report
    Quantum Nonlocality and SVMs: Classification of Local Hidden States via 9D Fano Feature Representations
    Tamar Noselidze
    Research Technical Report, UNICAMP & Sorbonne University, 2026
    Abs Bib Code

    We engineer a support vector machine (SVM) pipeline to classify Local Hidden States (LHS) and quantum steerability boundaries by compressing 2-qubit density matrices into a 9-dimensional Fano feature representation. By overcoming linear programming bottlenecks via MOSEK-accelerated convex optimisation, we extract analytical algebraic boundaries characterising quantum nonlocality.

  1. Thesis
    Adversarial Examples Against Vision Transformers
    Tamar Noselidze
    Charles University (Supervised by Doc. Mgr. Martin Pilát, Ph.D.), Jun 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code

    Deep learning models have been remarkably successful in image classification tasks, although they still remain vulnerable to adversarial examples. These are modified input images, designed in a special way to fool victim models into making incorrect predictions. Among adversarial methods, we focus on patch-based attacks as they pose security risks, due to their physical applicability in real-world scenarios. This thesis explores the effectiveness of patch-based adversarial attacks against Vision Transformers, as well as their transferability across model architectures. We begin with evaluating the effectiveness of several white-box gradient-based methods on Vision Transformers and compare it to Convolutional Neural Networks. We then replicate an existing patch-based attack, called G-Patch and further build on it. Our contributions include a novel Mini-Patch method with three deployment strategies: random placement, corner-point and token-replacement. We carried out extensive experiments in each setting and learned that corner-point patches perform comparably to state-of-the-art methods. We further investigate transferability in both G-Patch and Mini-Patch settings, which revealed that mixed-architecture ensembles help with generalisability of adversarial examples. Overall, our work provides insights into ViT model vulnerabilities and how they compare to CNNs, as well as attack transferability.
